# NEXA-M2-009 classroom load test, Pomnsoe school server (Jetson)

Host: NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX 16 GB (Super dev kit), 8 cores, Ollama 0.32.1, llama3.1:8b-instruct-q5_K_M,
nomic-embed-text. Package `edge_pomnsoe_v1` (Biology and Physics, 59 sources, 1,135 chunks), answer route `local`.
The desktop and the v0 `nexa.service` were running throughout. Tool: `v1/tools/nexa_load.py` via
`v1/edge/deploy/loadtest.sh` (service restarted before every run; one warm-up question not counted).
Questions are M2-008 corpus items in distinct wordings; every answer is scored (expected source and key terms;
for off-curriculum questions, no curriculum answer).

## Runs

"Before" = no answer queue, gateway timeout 60 s, answer timeout 120 s, Ollama defaults.
"Queue" = `NEXA_GENERATION_CONCURRENCY=2`, `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=2`, queue wait up to 600 s, timeouts 300 s.
"Queue + lean" = queue, plus `OLLAMA_FLASH_ATTENTION=1`, `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0`, `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=4096`,
`OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS=2`, `NEXA_GENERATION_OUTAGE_WAIT_S=120`.

## Findings

1. Without a queue, simultaneous questions all reach the model, slow each other down and hit the timeout:
   half of a class of 30 and almost three quarters of 100 got "answer unavailable".
2. With the queue every student in a class of 30 asking at once was answered (all within 4.2 minutes).
3. The 100-at-once run with the queue ran the Jetson out of memory: the kernel killed `llama-server`
   (Nov 15 11:57:47, 12:22:27, 12:29:24 UTC; swap 0 free). While Ollama restarted, the gateway's circuit
   breaker failed every queued answer at once. Fixed by the lean Ollama settings (no kill since) and by the
   outage wait (#108). Memory after the lean 100-student run: 0.9 GB available, 2.4 GB swap in use.
4. The MAXN_SUPER power mode made no measurable difference against 40 W.
5. The Jetson writes about 5–7 answers a minute. A burst of 100 distinct questions needs about 15 minutes;
   with the 10-minute queue limit, the last 18 got "answer unavailable". This is the hardware ceiling.
6. Answers are not less accurate under load (72–84% correct, against 77.6% for the M2-008 live suite on the DGX).
7. The answer cache matters: in the realistic run 35 of 100 answers were served instantly.

## Settings kept on the Jetson

`/etc/nexa-edge/edge.env`: `NEXA_GENERATION_CONCURRENCY=2`, `NEXA_GENERATION_QUEUE_TIMEOUT_S=600`,
`NEXA_ANSWER_TIMEOUT=300`, `NEXA_GATEWAY_TIMEOUT=300`, `NEXA_GENERATION_OUTAGE_WAIT_S=120`,
`NEXA_GENERAL_MODEL=nexa-chat-precise`. `/etc/systemd/system/ollama.service.d/nexa-edge.conf`: the lean
Ollama settings above. Power mode MAXN_SUPER.

## Capacity statement

On its own, one Jetson school server serves a class of about 30 asking at once, or 100 students asking over
a lesson (all answered, 9 in 10 within about 2 minutes). A burst of 100 distinct questions at the same moment
needs help from core NEXA (DGX), which is the next step.
